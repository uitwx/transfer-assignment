### A short GIT story

Tom had been hired as the new developer at a small company, where the most important rule was simple: never deploy on Friday. Unfortunately, Tom had never been good at following rules. On his first Friday, he proudly announced, “I’ve written the new payment system!” His colleague looked worried. “Did you test it?” Tom replied, “Not exactly, but I did GIT commit.”

The deployment went badly. Within minutes, customers were receiving invoices for negative amounts, the coffee machine had started printing error messages, and the office printer somehow believed it was a web server. Tom stared at his screen in horror. “Don’t worry,” he said. “I can fix it with GIT.” His colleague sighed. “You mean Git?” Tom nodded confidently. “Yes. Whatever that is.”

Tom opened the version control program and began examining the history. Luckily, every change had been recorded. He found the exact commit that had broken everything. “Look!” he shouted. “We can go back to the old version!” His colleague smiled. “Finally, you understand why we use GIT.” Tom replied, “Of course. I thought it was mainly for making programmers feel powerful.”
